## Vizcom workflow

What the tool does with a photo: it reads the shapes and shading in whatever is on the canvas and redraws them in the style you pick. A photo is the strongest base you can give it, because it already has depth and volume; a line sketch has to be shaded before the wash and chalkboard ground will take. That is why the Neemrana render worked.

The order that gets the Neemrana result every time:

1. Set the canvas at 3:2 before importing anything (1536 × 1024 or larger). Vizcom renders the whole canvas; cropping afterwards throws away resolution you paid for.
2. Import the reference photo as a layer. Use the transform tool to make the subject fill the frame. Vizcom's own guidance is that the product should dominate the frame and that larger subjects come out more detailed.
3. Paint out anything you do not want redrawn before you render: logos, wordmarks, licence plates, faces, signage. A flat grey brush stroke is enough. What is not on the canvas cannot come out in the render. This is the cheapest way to enforce the no-logo and no-face rules; asking for it in the prompt is the unreliable way.
4. Attach the Neemrana render as a reference image (docs: "Reference Images influence style and finish but do not override your base design's core structure unless drawing influence is set very high"). Set reference strength high. This is what keeps the twenty slots looking like one series.
5. Prompt. Lead with the subject, because Vizcom weights the first words most, then the view, then the setting, then the style. Their template is "(material object), (view), (setting), (brand DNA), (other properties)". So the prompts below are written subject first and the style tail last. Keep it short; their docs say short prompts do as well as long ones. Emphasis syntax if a render keeps ignoring something: `(charcoal sketch:1.3)`, `(chalkboard ground:1.2)`; range 0.5 to 1.5.
6. Drawing Influence. This is the slider that decides how much of the photo survives. High keeps the photo's geometry and just restyles it; low lets the prompt redraw the scene. For a vehicle you want recognisable (883, Sprint, Street 750, Redshift) run it high, around 70 to 85. For a scene where the photo is only a layout guide (dealer floor, keynote hall, trading floor) run it 40 to 60 so the prompt can change the people, weather and props. If a render looks like a filtered photo rather than a drawing, drop it ten points and go again.
7. Style. There is no "charcoal" palette. "Automotive Exterior" at a low palette influence gives what their docs call "lightly shaded sketches"; that plus the reference image and the prompt tail is the closest built-in route. Once you have four to six renders you like, train a custom style on them (4 to 30 images, consistent lighting and material, simple backgrounds, no mixing sketches with photos). A custom style is the proper fix for series consistency; Vizcom says that is what it is for. Publishing the style to a workspace library is Enterprise only, but training and using one on your own account is not.
8. Refine and Modify. Do not re-render the whole frame to fix one corner. Select the area and use Modify with a short prompt. Use it to knock out a face that resolved, a badge that survived, or a background that got busy.
9. Angle. Vary it from the reference. Three-quarter front from the reference's side view, or the reverse. Two reasons: the render is a drawing of a scene, not a copy of a photo, and it removes any question about the reference photo's licence. All the references below are CC or public domain anyway, but the rule holds.
10. Export at full canvas size as JPEG. I resize to 1400 wide, quality 82, and embed.

On licences: the reference is never published, so its licence only matters if the render is a close copy. Keep the angle change and the style change and it is not. I still chose CC and public-domain references so there is nothing to argue about.

## Logos

No Bar & Shield, no wordmarks. Harley's media kit (h-dmediakit.com) is gated to accredited journalists, editorial use only, and its click-through forbids reproducing anything "for any commercial purpose aside from publication." The corporate terms of use say the marks may not be used without express written consent, and there is no public logo library with a link-back licence, from Harley or from Hero, KYMCO or LiveWire. The text pill on each page names the company, which is fine. If you want the mark itself, that is a written request to Harley-Davidson Corporate Communications, and an independent brief is not what the kit's licence covers.
