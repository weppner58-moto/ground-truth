# Ground Truth on LinkedIn: launch plan

Written 15 September 2026. Covers the three issues that exist, twelve carousel posts plus the posts between them, from the first profile change to the last measurement. It builds on the exposure guide of 10 September; where the two differ, this one is current. The post texts themselves are in the three draft docs (linkedin-post-drafts, -02, -03) and are not repeated here.

One correction to something said in conversation: the link does not go in the first comment. That pattern is detected and penalised the same as a link in the body. The link goes in the body, once, at the end, and the profile carries it permanently.

## The sequence

Three issues, four parts each, two posts a week, Tuesday and Thursday, 8 to 10 am Pacific. Between issues, one Tuesday with a single short post, so the feed has a week of your name on something small before the next series, and so registrations from the last issue have time to arrive before you judge it. Six weeks of series, two weeks of gaps, eight weeks in all.

No. 01 goes first because it is the one with the number everyone has already seen, No. 02 second because it is the parent's arithmetic and needs No. 01's reader to already know what LiveWire costs, No. 03 last because it is the thesis and it lands best on people who have read the other two. No. 03 also sits after the Q3 calls on purpose: Harley reports in the last week of October or the first of November (2022: 26 Oct; 2024: 24 Oct; 2025: the 10-Q came 5 Nov) and LiveWire a few days later. Whatever the calls say about the Sprint's plant and price, LiveWire's financing and the H2 loss goes into No. 03 Part 4 before it posts, and into the gap post before No. 03 starts. Confirm the dates when Harley announces them, usually two weeks ahead, and if a call lands on a series day, hold the part to the following Tuesday. A fresh part beats a stale one.

## Before 22 September

The profile is where the link lives, so it gets fixed first. Headline: what you are and where the work is, in one line, with contactpatchadvisory.com in it. The contact section carries the URL. The About section ends with the Ground Truth line and the URL. Featured holds two items: the current issue's brief page and the landing page, pinned; swap the first when the next issue starts. Every click from the profile costs nothing in reach.

Start a LinkedIn newsletter called Ground Truth before the first post. Subscribers get every issue delivered by LinkedIn with no feed between you and them; you do not get their emails, so it is a second channel, not the list. ## How each post is built

Document post, PDF uploaded natively, from the desktop site. Not a link to the PDF, not images, not a video. The document title field is "Ground Truth No. 01 · Part 1 · The Growth"; LinkedIn shows it above the carousel and indexes it.

The first slide is the post. It decides the dwell time that decides everything after. It has to be a number or a sentence, not a title, and all twelve now are: "5 years in. 1% of plan." "From $29,799 to $4,999." "Two contracts, one trap." "It was decided in Nov 2025." "Back to the Bricks. Down to breakeven." "$180M a year, traded for $1.25B once." "Guided to lose more than the motor company makes." "Five pillars. Six targets. No electric." "Sixteen outside moves in 66 years." "$125M and $268M, with the same sentence." "21,633 to 653." "1960: an Aermacchi. 2026: a Hero." The chart or sketch sits under each.

The route card is the second slide of every part, so a reader who stops early still knows there is a series and where the rest is.

The text above the document opens with the number or the sentence, never with context. The drafts already do this. Trim each to under 350 words before posting; cut anything the slides already say. No hashtags. No emoji. No "I'm excited to share." Disclosure line on every post, and the Fenigstein disclosure on No. 03 Part 3.

The link: once, last line, the short form with the part tag, `contactpatchadvisory.com/01?p=1`. It costs reach on that post and you take the cost, because the reader who has just spent ninety seconds in the document is the one who registers. No second link, no link in the first comment, no "link in bio." When someone asks for the brief in the comments, reply with the link; a reply with a link is not penalised and it is one more comment on your own post.

Tag nobody at Harley-Davidson or LiveWire. Tag a person only if they have said they want to be in the conversation. A tagged stranger who does not respond is a signal against the post.

Post it yourself at the time, not scheduled. Then the hour.

## The first hour

Stay at the keyboard for sixty minutes. Every comment gets a reply that adds something: a filing reference, a page number, a second number, a concession where one is due. Two lines. "Thanks for reading" is worse than no reply. The four people you named ask their questions in the first twenty minutes; answer them the same way you would answer a stranger.

Read the execution notes in the draft doc for that part before posting. They list the objections that are coming and the answer to each. The rules that hold across every post: answer facts with filings, never argue tone, do not speculate past what the brief states as inferred, and when someone from Harley or LiveWire engages, that is a corrections-log event before it is a reply.

Do not edit the post in the first hour. Do not delete and repost if the first thirty minutes are quiet. If a post is dead at sixty minutes, it stays up, the next post keeps its date, and the first slide of the next part gets another look.

## After the hour

At 24 and 48 hours, record the numbers. At the end of each week, read the comments again and write down every objection you did not have a clean answer for. Those are the open items for the next issue and the material for the gap post.

Reply to comments that arrive on day two and three; the feed can resurface a document post for weeks, and a thread that is still moving is what it resurfaces.

## Measurement

One sheet, one row per post, filled in by hand. Impressions at one hour and at 48 hours. Reactions, comments, comments from people you do not know, saves, shares. Profile views the following day. Newsletter subscribers. Registrations by campaign tag from the CSV at `contactpatchadvisory.com/groundtruth/registrations?token=...`, where `campaign` reads `01-1` through `03-4`, `g` for the gap posts, `q3` for the call reactions. The one-hour impression number says whether the first slide worked. The registration column says which part sells the site. After No. 01 you will know which of the two matters more to you; the plan above assumes registrations.

Two things to decide from No. 01's numbers before No. 02 posts. If the four linked posts run far below what your unlinked comments and gap post reach, drop the link from Parts 2 and 3 of No. 02 and let the profile and the replies carry it; keep it on Parts 1 and 4. If Part 1's one-hour number is the best of the four, the series is front-loaded and No. 02's Part 1 should carry the strongest single number in that issue, which is the subsidiary guided to lose more than the motor company makes.

## When something happens

A journalist picks it up: reply, offer the filings and the corrections log, do not pitch the next issue. Log the mention.

Someone disputes a number: reply with the filing, accession number and page. If they are right, say so in the reply, fix the brief, log it in corrections, and say in the next post that it was corrected and by whom. This is the whole point of the corrections log and it is the most credible thing you can do in public.

Harley or LiveWire responds, in any form: screenshot it, log it, reply once with the document reference, and write the next gap post around it.

A post goes far wider than the others: do not change the schedule. Reply to everything for two days. Pin that post to Featured until the next issue starts.

A Q3 call contradicts a brief: the reaction post says so first, before anyone else does. The reader you want is the one who sees you concede before you argue.

## What not to do

No company page; Contact Patch Advisory stays a name on your profile until the list is worth a page. No pods, no asking for likes, no reposting your own post the same week. No link in the first comment. No hashtags beyond one, inline. No posting the same carousel twice. No AI-cadence text; the drafts are in your voice and the dash rule stays. No engaging on what Hero, KYMCO or any private party intends beyond what they have said in print. No replies to tone; the filings are the reply.
